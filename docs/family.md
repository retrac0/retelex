# The N = 1 / 3 / 7 family — framings and line rates

> **Superseded in part by `spec.md` (2026-09-16).** The per-rate audio figures stand.
> Rates are now reached from one 4.096 MHz crystal by a rate strap rather than by
> crystal swaps, and the modes table with noise margins is spec §2.1.

Generated by `family.py`; quality measured by `seven.py` on seven independent
LibriSpeech talkers, 30 s each. Reach is scaled analysis, not simulation — see
§5.

---

## 1. Framings

`2 + 2N` bits. Payload at counts `0 … flen-3`, framing at the last two, S = 0
and T = 1. Signalling and voice alternate so that no two adjacent interior bits
are static, which is what makes start/stop framing safe (spec §2.1).

| N | frame | counter | wrong alignments | layout |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 bits | 2-bit | 3 | `[A1][C1][S][T]` |
| **3** | **8 bits** | **3-bit** | **7** | `[A1][C1][A2][C2][A3][C3][S][T]` |
| 7 | 16 bits | 4-bit | 15 | `[A1][C1]…[A7][C7][S][T]` |

`2 + 2N` is a power of two only at N = 1, 3, 7 and 15. Only then is the whole
crystal-to-frame chain a set of binary taps on one 74HC4040, with no decoded
modulus and no sync-clear gate.

## 2. Line rates

The frame clock **is** the codec clock, so the CVSD rate sets everything:

```
line rate  = codec rate x frame length        symbol rate = 2 x line rate
oscillator = 16 x symbol rate                 osc / frame = 32 x frame length
```

`osc / frame` is therefore `32 × flen` — binary whenever the frame is, and
**independent of the codec rate**. Changing the CVSD rate is a crystal swap and
new codec RC values; not one gate changes.

| N | frame | CVSD | line rate | symbol | oscillator | osc÷frame | fundamental | reach |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4 | 16k | 64 kbps | 0.128 M | 2.048 MHz | 2⁷ | 32 kHz | 3.40 km |
| 1 | 4 | 32k | 128 kbps | 0.256 M | 4.096 MHz | 2⁷ | 64 kHz | 2.40 km |
| 1 | 4 | 64k | 256 kbps | 0.512 M | 8.192 MHz | 2⁷ | 128 kHz | 1.70 km |
| 3 | 8 | 16k | 128 kbps | 0.256 M | 4.096 MHz | 2⁸ | 64 kHz | 2.40 km |
| **3** | **8** | **32k** | **256 kbps** | **0.512 M** | **8.192 MHz** | **2⁸** | **128 kHz** | **1.70 km** |
| 3 | 8 | 64k | 512 kbps | 1.024 M | 16.384 MHz | 2⁸ | 256 kHz | 1.20 km |
| 7 | 16 | 16k | 256 kbps | 0.512 M | 8.192 MHz | 2⁹ | 128 kHz | 1.70 km |
| 7 | 16 | 32k | 512 kbps | 1.024 M | 16.384 MHz | 2⁹ | 256 kHz | 1.20 km |
| 7 | 16 | 64k | 1024 kbps | 2.048 M | 32.768 MHz | 2⁹ | 512 kHz | 0.85 km |

The committed build is the bold row.

## 3. Quality per channel

Measured codec-alone at each rate on seven independent talkers, 30 s, compared
at 8 kHz:

| CVSD rate | mean segSNR | worst talker | spec §9 (old 4-file set) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 kbps | 10.80 dB | 9.02 dB | 10.8 dB |
| **32 kbps** | **17.40 dB** | **14.91 dB** | **17.9 dB** |
| 64 kbps | 23.28 dB | 19.69 dB | 24.0 dB |

Roughly 6 dB per doubling with no knee, as spec §9 says. The independent
material comes in 0.5–0.7 dB below the original figures at the higher rates,
so the spec's numbers were mildly optimistic but not wrong.

**Per-talker spread is larger than the carrier's own contribution.** The worst
talker sits ~2.5 dB below the mean at every rate, and it tracks crest factor
(9.02 dB at crest 15.4, 12.37 dB at crest 8.6). Specify against the worst
talker, not the mean.

## 4. Equal-line-rate groups — the trade that matters

Because line rate is `codec rate × frame length`, different (N, rate) pairs
land on the same line rate. **Those share the pair, the driver, the receiver,
the oscillator and the reach** — they differ only in frame width and codec RC
values, so they are strap options on one design rather than different designs.

### 256 kbps — the committed PHY

| variant | channels | per-channel | total |
|---|---|---|---|
| N=7 @ 16 kbps | 7 | 10.80 dB | 7 poor channels |
| **N=3 @ 32 kbps** | **3** | **17.40 dB** | **the committed build** |
| N=1 @ 64 kbps | 1 | 23.28 dB | 1 good channel |

### 512 kbps — 16.384 MHz, reach 1.20 km

| variant | channels | per-channel |
|---|---|---|
| N=7 @ 32 kbps | 7 | 17.40 dB |
| N=3 @ 64 kbps | 3 | 23.28 dB |

### 128 kbps — 4.096 MHz, reach 2.40 km

| variant | channels | per-channel |
|---|---|---|
| N=3 @ 16 kbps | 3 | 10.80 dB |
| N=1 @ 32 kbps | 1 | 17.40 dB |

**The rule is 2.33× the channels for −5.9 dB, at constant reach.** Whether that
is worth it is a plant question. Note that it is not a smooth trade: the family
only offers N = 1, 3, 7, so the steps are coarse.

## 5. Receiver timing

The monostable is 0.75 of a cell, and the ±13% window of spec §5.2 is a
percentage, so both scale with the line rate:

| N | CVSD | cell | half-cell | monostable | ±13% window |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 32k | 3.91 µs | 1953 ns | 2930 ns | ±381 ns |
| 3 | 64k | 1.95 µs | 977 ns | 1465 ns | ±190 ns |
| 7 | 32k | 1.95 µs | 977 ns | 1465 ns | ±190 ns |
| 7 | 64k | 0.98 µs | 488 ns | 732 ns | **±95 ns** |

A 1% resistor and a 2% C0G capacitor give ±3%, which clears ±13% everywhere in
the table. The absolute figure is what gets awkward: at N=7 @ 64 kbps the
window is ±95 ns, where layout strays and the '4040's own ripple skew (~110 ns)
stop being negligible.

**Reach figures are scaled from the spec's single "order 1.7 km" anchor** by
√f, since attenuation on 24 AWG goes as √f in the skin-effect region. That
anchor is itself unmeasured. The ratios between rows are far more trustworthy
than any absolute value, and the whole column needs a bench measurement.

## 6. Where the family stops

**N = 7 @ 64 kbps is the practical corner.** Everything below it is ordinary;
it is not:

- 32.768 MHz into a 74HC4040 ripple counter, where accumulated stage delay
  stops being a footnote
- 2.048 Mbaud with a 488 ns half-cell into outside plant — a different
  transmission problem, not a faster version of the same one
- a ±95 ns monostable window
- 0.85 km reach, which is short enough to question the point

N = 15 doubles all of that again and needs two 74HC259s per bit type. If more
capacity is ever wanted at that end of the table, it wants a different line
code, not a longer frame or a faster codec.

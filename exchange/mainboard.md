# The exchange — the mainboard: what runs between the blocks

`fabric.md` §2 lists the bus in one table and leaves its physical form
open: "everything below is on the mainboard or a ribbon between blocks", with
"sixteen highways across eight blocks on a ribbon or a mainboard" among the
things not measured. This document closes that. It is the signal list in full,
the two cables that carry it — **fabric cable A** and **control cable B** — what
decides a block's number, what a cut or an absent part reads as, and the
electrical argument — which turns out to be the easy part.

**The control bus is serial** (§2.3): the marker's whole output is one sixteen-bit
word broadcast on one wire, and the twenty-four parallel signals this document
first listed are seven. That is the only part of §2 that is not `fabric.md`
§2 as it stands.

> **Status: proposed; the timing measured by the same method as `fabric.md`
> §4.** `exch.py mainboard` adds the clock's own flight down the cable to that
> section's 400 000 draws. The line's capacitance, edge rate and drive current
> are arithmetic from the ribbon's geometry. Crosstalk is an estimate and is
> named as one (§9). Nothing has been drawn and nothing has been on a bench.

---

## 1. The one thing that decides the shape

A ribbon is a bus of identical conductors, and every connector crimped along it
sees the same signals. Almost all of this bus wants exactly that — the clocks,
the sixteen highways, the marker's control lines are the same at every block.

**Three signals are not.** Block *h* drives highway `HC[h]` and `HA[h]` and
answers to `/BLK` line *h*, and that is the whole of a block's identity: it is why
`fabric.md` §3.1 can say a block "needs no address and no comparator —
layout, not logic". A plain daisy-chained ribbon cannot give it, because every
tap is the same. Making a block work it out instead costs a '138 on three
tied ID pins, eight individually-enabled tri-states for `HC` and eight for
`HA`: **about six packages a block, forty-eight in the exchange, 7 % of the
fabric, to buy back something the layout was giving away free.**

Since §2.3 made the control bus serial, **`BLK[0..7]` is not on the cable at
all.** Every block decodes the word's `DST[5:3]` through a '138 of its own, and the
**block shunt** picks one of those eight lines exactly as it used to pick one of
eight conductors. The block shunt, its three columns and its silkscreen are
unchanged, and the cable is now identical at every tap without exception. **That
'138 is not the one this section has just refused**: no ID pins, no comparator,
the same part doing the same thing in all eight blocks, and the shunt is still
what says which line is mine. The decoder moved from the marker's end of the wire
to the block's, and nothing learned its own name on the way.

So the split is:

```
  fabric cable A + control cable B, identical taps  ─── 38 signals, the same at every block
                        │
                  the MAINBOARD at each tap ── a passive board: the cables' headers, the block's
                        │                      connector, and the **block shunt**: three 2 × 8
                        │                      columns (HC / HA / `/BLK`)
                        │
                     the block  ─── HC_OUT, HA_OUT, /BLK: three pins, and the shunt column says which
```

**The block's number is three shunts in one column.** The mainboard brings the
eight `HC` conductors to the top row of a 2 × 8 header and busses the block's
`HC_OUT` pin along the bottom row; a shunt in column *h* makes it block *h*'s
highway. Two more headers do the same for `HA_OUT` and for `/BLK`, which runs the
other way. Silkscreen 0–7 across them and a block's number is three jumpers in a
labelled column, set once when the subrack is wired. No part, no logic, and the
mainboard is one board design wherever it sits.

This is the same bargain as `mf8-backplane.md` §3's "no slot-ID pins", one level
up: **the cable is common, the board is position-wired, and the card knows
nothing.**

## 2. The signals

Thirty-eight, in three groups. All 74HC at 5 V. The groups are by **who drives
them, with one exception**: `RBT` belongs to §2.1's set because it is what that
set is — one fabric-wide reference every block receives and nobody switches — but
it is generated by the ringback tone card, not by the clock unit (§2.1).
`fabric.md` §2 is the same list except for §2.3, which replaces its twenty
parallel latch-write signals with a serial word; `LINE_HB`, `RX_OCT` and `/CON` are
new here and §8 says why.

### 2.1 The fabric-wide references — 15, of which the clock card drives 14

| signal | rate | what |
|---|---|---|
| `HCLK` | 512 kHz | the cell clock, high for the first half of a cell (1 953 ns). **The one signal on the bus whose edges are load-bearing**: through each block's '138 it becomes every port's capture strobe |
| `SLOT[2:0]` | 512 kHz | the cell number, changing at the start of the cell. Addresses every commutator and the trunk frame mux (`fabric.md` §1.1) |
| `SLOTQ[2:0]` | 512 kHz | the same count a quarter cell late, from the '175 on the 1.024 MHz tap. Addresses every strobe decoder and nothing else (`fabric.md` §3.2) |
| `LINE_HB` | 1.024 MHz | the tap the '175 clocks `SLOTQ` from. **The fastest thing on the bus.** Also the trunk encoder's half-bit clock on a fused MF8 link (`mf8-spec.md` §9.1) |
| `TX_CLK` | 64 kHz | the frame clock. Every card's `RX_CLK` pin is this signal |
| `CLK_8K` | 8 kHz | the exchange name for socket `TX_OCT` / a3. Scanner and monitors step on it; a port visit is one period (125 µs), filled by the 16-bit word at 128 kHz (`extensions.md` §2.2) |
| `RX_OCT` | 8 kHz | `CLK_8K` one frame late, from one flip-flop on the clock card (`fabric.md` §3.4). A card's `RX_OCT` pin |
| `FILL` | 32 kHz | the `1010` idle pattern: what an unfitted port, an unfitted block and a cleared latch all hear |
| `RBT` | 64 kb/s | the ringback tone as bits, substituted ahead of a ringing port's commutator input. **The one line in this group the clock card does not drive** — see below |
| `/RING_CAD` | 2 s / 4 s | the ring cadence, low true |
| `TICK` | 1 kHz | the marker's and the registers' time base |

**`RBT` is the clock unit's conceptually and the tone card's electrically.** It
is a bussed reference like `FILL` — one conductor, every block receiving, nothing
switching it — which is why it is listed here, and why `fabric.md` §4
carries it beside them while naming the tone card as its driver. But a ringback
tone is an **analog oscillator through a CVSD encoder** (`features.md` §2),
and that has no business on a board which is otherwise a crystal and a divider
chain: no `±12 V`, no LM13700, no frequency pots on the clock card.

So the ringback tone card generates it and drives this conductor through a
buffer on the card — ⅛ of a 74HC244 and 33 Ω, out of the card's rear connector
and punched to `RBT` in the wiring field (`tone-card.md` §1, §5). On this bus
`RBT` then behaves exactly as a highway does: **one driver, about ten receivers,
and pulled to `FILL` through 4.7 kΩ at the clock card when nothing drives it**
(§2.2's rule, and `mf8-backplane.md` §6's idiom). An exchange with no ringback card
fitted has `RBT` at `FILL`, and a caller hears silence while the far phone rings
(`mvp.md`) — which is the documented behaviour already, and is now the same
mechanism as an unfitted block rather than a special case.

**The clock card's part in `RBT` is the 4.7 kΩ and the fabric-cable pin.** It
terminates the line and does not populate a '244 gate for it.

### 2.2 The highways — 16

| signal | driver | loads | what |
|---|---|---|---|
| `HC[0..7]` | block *h* drives wire *h*, through its shunt | every block, plus the marker's probe | eight ports a wire, one a cell. The whole switch |
| `HA[0..7]` | the same | every block; the marker's, the registers' and the monitors' probes | the signalling highways. `HA` is also the M highway the probes read |

Each highway carries one driver and about ten receivers. **A highway with no
driver — an unfitted block, or a missing shunt — is pulled to `FILL` through
4.7 kΩ at the clock card**, exactly as an empty MF8 slot is pulled
(`mf8-backplane.md` §6), so it reads as CVSD silence rather than as noise.
`HA` is pulled to ground: on-hook.

### 2.3 The control bus — 7, multi-master

Driven by the marker, or by the operator's console when it takes the bus.

**The marker's whole output is one sixteen-bit word, broadcast to every block at
once.** Twenty of the twenty-four signals this section first listed were a single
latch write held on twenty wires; they are sixteen bits on one wire now, and the
block that answers is the one its shunt column names.

| signal | direction | what |
|---|---|---|
| `SDATA` | marker or console → blocks | the word, MSB first |
| `SCLK` | the same | shifts it. **128 kHz** (gated per word, or free-running off `HCLK` ÷ 4); §2.3.2 |
| `WR` | the same | the write. Gated with `/BLK` and the guard bit in the block's write decoder, where `BLK[h]`·`WR` used to be |
| `/RESET` | marker or power-on → blocks | clears every latch: every port listening to 00. **The signal on this bus a glitch can hurt most** (§7) |
| `PB[1:0]` | the selected block → marker, tri-state | `BUSY`, and the card's **16-bit** word at **128 kHz** over the visit. **Pulled LOW at the marker** (§6) |
| `/CON` | console → marker | the console has the bus; the marker's drivers go high-impedance. One '244 enable |

#### 2.3.1 The word

| bit | field | what |
|---|---|---|
| 15 | `G` | guard, **always 1**. ANDed into `WR` at every block: a word that does not carry it cannot write |
| 14 | — | spare. Parity over 13–0 if the bench asks for it (§7) |
| 13–11 | `DST[5:3]` | the block. Into the block's '138; the shunt picks `/BLK` |
| 10–8 | `DST[2:0]` | the port in it. The write decoder's address, unchanged |
| 7–2 | `SRC[5:0]` | the source port, **as dialled** (`protocol.md` §5) |
| 1 | `BSY` | busy |
| 0 | `CLM` | a claimed trunk's backward A |

Both halves are port numbers as dialled — where to write, and what to write — so
the word is `h s` twice and two flags. And **bits 7–0 are the port's 74HC273
verbatim**: `SRC`, `BSY` and `CLM` are eight bits because the latch is eight bits.
One shift register holds the address and the other holds exactly what lands in
the latch, which is why sixteen was the number to aim at.

`BLK[0..7]` as eight one-hot conductors was the only fat on the old bus: eight
bits to say one of eight things. Sent as three and decoded at the block, it pays
for `DST` and `SRC` together and leaves two bits over.

#### 2.3.2 At the block: two '164, one '138, one gate

| part | what |
|---|---|
| 2 × 74HC164 | the sixteen bits, shifted by `SCLK`. **No output latch**, so no '595: nothing reads the outputs until `WR`, and the garbage that ripples through between writes is the same garbage the parallel bus carried between writes |
| 1 × 74HC138 | `DST[5:3]` into eight lines, out to the mainboard's `/BLK` shunt column and back as `/BLK` |
| 1 gate | `G` · `WR`, into the write decoder's enable where `BLK[h]`·`WR` used to go |
| | **≈ 3¼ added, about one '244 freed: net ≈ 2¼ a block.** The control bus at a block has gone from fourteen inputs to five, and `fabric.md` §3.1 carries the same figure |

**Broadcast, not daisy-chained.** `SDATA` and `SCLK` reach every block, all eight
shift the same word and hold it, and only the block whose shunt matches `DST[5:3]`
answers `WR`. Chaining the registers through the blocks instead would be a
128-bit word and a position-dependent one — the opposite of §1's identical tap.

**The shift register has no phase, only content.** Its sixteen bits are right
whenever the marker has driven sixteen good bits in a row, so no block counts
anything and nothing needs a frame reference. `SCLK` may therefore free-run from
`HCLK` ÷ 4 on a jumper (128 kHz), at the cost of the control bus no longer standing
up without cable A; it is its own conductor here so that §3's bring-up order
survives.

**One rule.** `WR` must not land within a set-up window of an `SCLK` edge, or the
decoder reads the outputs while they move. At 128 kHz that window is 3.9 µs
wide, against a cable skew of 18 ns (§5.2). The marker pulses `WR` in the middle
of an `SCLK` half-period.

**Sixteen bits at 128 kHz is 125 µs**, one marker port visit, against a write
rate of a few a second. Marker and console both shift at this rate.
`protocol.md` §4 measured that the marker may write a latch at any instant
and needs no synchronisation to the frame, and that is untouched: the write is
still one strobe edge, and what it does at the worst moment is still repeat one
bit.

### 2.4 Not on the bus

**`−48 V` and `RING` never leave a subrack.** Ninety volts of ringing beside
sixteen highways on 0.05-inch pitch is not worth the one cable it saves; each
subrack takes its rails from its own PSU slot as `mf8-backplane.md` §8 has it,
and the mainboard carries `+5 V` and ground only.

**The MF8 trunk unit is not a bus tap either.** It is a **block adapter** on
its block's **block header** (`fabric.md` §5.2, `mf8-unit.md` §1): eight
card-side appearances, six live (1–3, 5–7), `MUTE_BLK` on the commons. It
does not tap `HC[h]` / `HA[h]` — those are the commutation of this block's
`C_TX` / `A_TX` after the unit has already driven them. Transmit needs the
ports' `C_RX` / `A_RX` off the header (`baseband.md` §6). It takes `HCLK`,
`LINE_HB`, `SLOT` and `CLK_8K` (`TX_OCT`) from the clock card through the
block (and the header commons). Fabric `LINE_HB` is also the line half-bit
clock at 1.024 MHz for the fused six-channel frame. So the cable has ten
taps — eight blocks, the clock card and common control — however many
trunks are fitted.

**Registers, resolvers and the marker share one tap.** A register's probe reads
its caller's hook off `HA` and regenerates its own strobes from `HCLK` and
`SLOTQ` with a '138 of its own; it does not need a block's strobe lines. The
digits it hands the marker stay on their own short ribbon between the two
(`marker.md` §4). Monitors sit on trunk blocks and need no tap at all.

## 3. The two cables

| | conductors | signals | carries |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — fabric cable A** | 64-way IDC | 31 + 1 spare | everything in §2.1 and §2.2 |
| **B — control cable B** | 16-way IDC | 7 + 1 spare | everything in §2.3 |

**A ground on every other conductor**, so every signal has a ground either side
and the cable's impedance is about 105 Ω. Both cables run the same route, in one
harness, with an IDC connector crimped at each of the ten taps: **one unbroken
conductor end to end, and no buffer anywhere between the clock card and a
block.** A repeater in the middle would add its own delay to the skew of §5, and
the skew is the only thing the cable costs.

Two cables rather than one because of what `fabric.md` §7 does at bring-up:
the clock card and a block come up on cable A alone, and the console and then the
marker arrive on cable B. It also puts the multi-master bus, which is the one with
a person on the end of it, on its own cable. **Serialising §2.3 made that split
nearly free** — cable B is a sixteen-way now, and the whole bus would fit one
64-way with spares if the bring-up order were worth less than a connector. It is
not.

### 3.1 Cable A, 64-way — odd pins signal, even pins ground

| pin | | pin | | pin | | pin | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | spare | 17 | `SLOTQ2` | 33 | `HC0` | 49 | `HA0` |
| 3 | **`HCLK`** | 19 | **`TX_CLK`** | 35 | `HC1` | 51 | `HA1` |
| 5 | `TICK` | 21 | `CLK_8K` | 37 | `HC2` | 53 | `HA2` |
| 7 | `SLOT0` | 23 | `RX_OCT` | 39 | `HC3` | 55 | `HA3` |
| 9 | `SLOT1` | 25 | `FILL` | 41 | `HC4` | 57 | `HA4` |
| 11 | `SLOT2` | 27 | `RBT` | 43 | `HC5` | 59 | `HA5` |
| 13 | `SLOTQ0` | 29 | `/RING_CAD` | 45 | `HC6` | 61 | `HA6` |
| 15 | `SLOTQ1` | 31 | **`LINE_HB`** | 47 | `HC7` | 63 | `HA7` |

The order is not arbitrary. **The three signals in bold are the only ones whose
edges matter**, and they are put eight conductors apart from each other and
sixteen from the highways, with the 1 kHz `TICK` and a spare as `HCLK`'s nearest
neighbours. The sixteen highways are bunched at the far end where the only thing
they can disturb is each other, which costs nothing (§7). `HCLK` is at pin 3 and
not pin 1 so that it has a ground on both sides.

### 3.2 Cable B, 16-way — odd pins signal, even pins ground

| pin | | pin | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | spare | 9 | **`WR`** |
| 3 | `SDATA` | 11 | `PB0` |
| 5 | **`SCLK`** | 13 | `PB1` |
| 7 | `/RESET` | 15 | `/CON` |

`SCLK` and `WR` are the two signals here whose edges matter, and `/RESET` — which
is filtered at every block and cannot be disturbed by either — sits between
them.

### 3.3 The block connector

Forty-eight signals: the 37 of §2 that reach a block (all of §2.1 and §2.2, and
all of §2.3 but `/CON`, which is console to marker only), `HC_OUT`, `HA_OUT` and
`/BLK` from the block shunt, and **the eight lines of the block's own `/BLK`
decoder on their way out to it** (§2.3.2). On a **96-way DIN 41612, row b entirely
ground**, rows a and c the forty-eight signals and `+5 V` — so every signal has a
ground pin beside it in the connector as it had in the cable. The serial control
bus cost the connector nothing: the thirteen parallel inputs it dropped pay for
the eight decoder lines and the four that replaced them. A block whose shared board (`fabric.md` §5.1) carries the cable headers and
the block shunt itself *is* the mainboard: there is no separate block
connector. The block ribbon still leaves that board toward the taps.

## 4. The block shunt

```
  mainboard, one per block position

        HC0 HC1 HC2 HC3 HC4 HC5 HC6 HC7        from fabric cable A, pins 33-47
         o   o   o   o   o   o   o   o
         |
         #   o   o   o   o   o   o   o         a shunt in column 3 -> this is block 3
         o- -o- -o- -o- -o- -o- -o- -o         all bussed to the block's HC_OUT pin
              ... the same for HA_OUT, and for /BLK the other way ...
              ... but /BLK's top row comes from the block's own '138 (2.3.2),
                  not from the cable: the block drives eight, takes one back ...
              0   1   2   3   4   5   6   7    silkscreen
```

Three shunts in one column — three 2 × 8 headers side by side (HC / HA /
`/BLK`). Set wrong, or left out, §6 says what happens.

**Since §2.3 all three columns run the same way about**: `HC_OUT` and `HA_OUT`
pick which conductor this block drives, and the `/BLK` column picks which line of
the block's own decoder is its own. The header is the same 2 × 8, the silkscreen
the same 0–7, and the only thing that changed is where the top row comes from.

## 5. Electrical — `exch.py mainboard`

Eight blocks a quarter of a metre apart: two metres of cable, 5.1 ns/m,
49 pF/m, ten taps.

### 5.1 The clock travels with the data, so the skew mostly cancels

A block's capture strobe comes from the `HCLK` that reached *it*, and the bit it
captures came from a block the same cable reached. Data arrives at listener *b*
at `dly(a) + flight(a,b)`; *b*'s strobe at `dly(b)`. The loss is the difference,
and it is **exactly zero whenever the driving block is nearer the clock card than
the listening one** — the flight cancels the skew term for term.

| clock card | worst loss | the pair |
|---|---|---|
| at one end of the cable | **17.9 ns** | block 7 driving, block 0 listening |
| **in the middle** | **8.9 ns** | block 7 driving, block 3 listening |

Put the clock card in the middle of the chain and the worst case halves, for the
cost of deciding which subrack it goes in. **Hold is never worse than with no
cable at all**: the same term that shortens set-up lengthens hold.

### 5.2 What that costs §4 of `fabric.md`

The worst pair on the worse placement, through the same 400 000 draws across the
74HC spread:

| | with the cable | no cable |
|---|---|---|
| set-up at the capture flip-flop | **725 ns** | 743 |
| hold | **933 ns** | 915 |

Two metres of ribbon costs 18 ns of a three-quarter-microsecond budget. The
*data's* own flight was already inside `fabric.md` §4's 12 ns `wire` term;
the clock's was not, and this is it.

**The §3.2 hazard is untouched.** `HCLK` and `SLOTQ` leave the clock card
together and arrive together, so their skew is conductor to conductor within one
ribbon — under 0.1 ns/m — and not the 5.1 ns/m of the cable itself. The address
still settles 367 ns before the enable.

### 5.3 The line, and why the cure for an edge is to slow it down

Two metres and eight taps is 193 pF and a 10.2 ns flight, 20.4 ns there and
back. A '244 into that load already gives a 17 ns edge, which is right at the
boundary where a line stops being lumped and starts ringing.

| series R at the driver | edge | of a cell |
|---|---|---|
| 33 Ω, as `mf8-backplane.md` §8 uses on 25 cm | 31 ns | 1.6 % |
| **100 Ω** | **59 ns** | **3.0 %** |
| 220 Ω | 110 ns | 5.7 % |

**100 Ω on every clock-card output.** It makes the edge three times the cable's
round trip, which is the definition of a line with nothing to settle, and it
spends 59 ns out of 725. At four metres, where the round trip is 41 ns, the same
resistor still gives 89 ns. A clock line draws C·V·f = 0.49 mA at 512 kHz and
0.99 mA at 1.024 MHz; a '244 sources 25 mA.

**This is the whole electrical story, and it is the point.** The fabric was
designed with three orders of magnitude in hand (`fabric.md` §6 refused to
spend 9 % of it to save seven packages a block), and a backplane problem that
would be delicate at 50 MHz is, at 512 kHz with a 750 ns budget, solved by one
resistor pack and a ground between every pair.

## 6. What an absent or wrong part reads as

| | on the bus | what the exchange does |
|---|---|---|
| **a block not fitted** | its `HC` pulled to `FILL`, its `HA` to ground, `PB` pulled low | eight ports that read unequipped, never hunted, heard as silence by anyone pointed at them. The other seven blocks are untouched |
| **its three shunts left out** | the same | the same. **A block with no number is simply not heard**, and every port in it still listens perfectly well to everything else — which is a usable state to bring a block up in |
| **two blocks on the same column** | two '244s on one highway, two `PB` drivers on one select, and both blocks answering one `/BLK` — though each off its own decoder, so there is no contention there | contention on the highway: a broken `1010`, garbage in both blocks' words, and every write meant for one landing in both. Caught at `fabric.md` §7 step 2, which scopes exactly this pattern. Both blocks hot but not damaged |
| **cable B unplugged at a block** | `/RESET`, `WR`, `SCLK` and `SDATA` all pulled low at the block | that block clears every latch and takes no writes: every one of its ports listening to 00, silent, no call held. The clean failure, and **doubly so now** — with `SDATA` low the shift register fills with zeros, the guard bit `G` reads 0, and `WR` is inhibited even if it were to arrive |
| **cable A unplugged at a block** | `HCLK` and `SLOT` pulled low at the block | the strobes freeze, every capture flip-flop holds its last bit, the commutator puts port *h*0 on the highway continuously. Dead and obviously dead; nothing else is hurt |
| **the clock card pulled** | no clocks at all | the exchange stops. Nothing rings, nothing releases, every latch keeps what it had until `/RESET`. As `mf8-backplane.md` §6's "trunk card absent" |
| **a shunt in the `/BLK` row only** | the block answers the marker but drives no highway | its ports read and write correctly and are heard as `FILL`. The confusing one, and the reason the three shunts are one column on one silkscreen |

The polarity of every pull is chosen so that **absent reads as empty, not as
busy**: `PB` is pulled *low*, so a block the marker cannot select reads as eight
unequipped slots — class 000, never hunted, `BUSY` clear — rather than as eight
phantom busy lines, which is what a pull-up would give.

## 7. What a glitch costs, which is the real question

A backplane is judged by what its worst noise event does. Take every signal in
turn and ask what one spurious edge on it does to a call in progress:

| signal | a spurious edge does | costs |
|---|---|---|
| `HC`, `HA` | nothing. They are sampled 750 ns after they change; a glitch is 20 ns long | nothing |
| `PB` | nothing. The marker reads a word over a whole 125 µs visit, and re-reads it 8 ms later | nothing |
| `FILL`, `RBT`, `TICK`, `/RING_CAD` | one wrong bit of tone, or a millisecond of cadence | nothing audible |
| `SLOT`, `SLOTQ` | a commutator or a decoder addressed wrong for 20 ns, while the strobe decoder is disabled | nothing |
| `SDATA` | nothing on its own. The word is read only at `WR`, and the marker drives the line for a whole `SCLK` period either side of each edge | nothing |
| `WR` | **writes the latch a second time, with the same data** | nothing |
| `SCLK` | **one extra shift: the next write goes to the wrong port, with the wrong source** | one call |
| `HCLK` | one extra strobe: **one port captures one wrong bit** | one bit |
| `TX_CLK` | one card samples twice, or sends one extra bit to its phone | one bit |
| `/RESET` | **every latch in the exchange cleared: every call in progress dropped** | the exchange |

One wrong CVSD bit is the event `mf8-backplane.md` §7 measured: a transient
30 dB below speech, settled in 1.2 ms, nine decibels below the codec's own
error, and the same thing a plesiochronous slip does every 156 ms on every line
of every MF8 shelf in the system. **So of thirty-eight signals, thirty-six cannot
lose a call, and the worst any of them can do is what the system already does to
itself twice a second.** The two that can are `/RESET`, which always could, and
`SCLK`, which is new and costs one call where `/RESET` costs the exchange.

That leaves `SCLK` and `/RESET`.

**`SCLK` is the one thing the serial bus has that the parallel one did not**, and
it is what eighteen conductors cost. A spurious edge shifts the word by one and
the next `WR` writes a wrong source into a wrong port: one call broken. That is
above the line this section has held everywhere else — one CVSD bit — and well
below `/RESET`'s, which drops every call at once. Three things bound it:

- **It does not persist.** A shift register has no stored phase, only content, so
  the next word re-aligns itself whatever happened to the last. The damage is
  exactly one bad write, not a bus out of step.
- **The port still recovers itself.** A wrongly-pointed port whose partner hangs
  up clears on its own detectors (`fabric.md` §3.5). Nothing is stuck and
  nothing waits on the marker noticing.
- **`SCLK` is already slow.** Sixteen bits at 128 kHz is 125 µs against a write
  rate of a few a second: four times fewer edges than `HCLK`, and the same
  cure `/RESET` gets below at a shorter constant — 1 kΩ and 1 nF is a
  microsecond, under 15 % of a 128 kHz edge.

**Measured** in the gates: `make sclk` injects one extra shift mid-word, sees the
write miss its port, and shows the next clean word re-aligns — idle-time edges
cost nothing.

**Bit 14 is left spare for parity rather than spent on it.** Checking fifteen bits
at every block is two 74HC280, sixteen packages in the exchange, against a fault
that costs one call; bit 15's guard costs one gate and catches the misalignments
that walk a 1 out of it. Which is right is a bench question and not an arithmetic
one, and §9 says so.

`/RESET` is not a fast signal — it comes from a pushbutton
and a power-on RC. **Filter it at every block**: a 10 kΩ and a 100 nF into one
gate of a '14, which each block already has for its supervision detectors
(`fabric.md` §3.5). A millisecond of time constant makes every glitch on the
bus invisible to it and costs a sixth of a package for the whole exchange.

That is the answer to whether a ribbon is a sensible way to build this. It is
sensible **because the fabric switches bits at 512 kHz with 750 ns of margin and
holds its state in a latch at every port**: there is no central memory for a
disturbance to corrupt, no clock recovery to lose, and no signal but the reset
whose worst case is worse than one CVSD bit.

## 8. Three signals this adds to `fabric.md` §2

- **`LINE_HB`, 1.024 MHz.** Fabric `LINE_HB` clocks the '175 that makes `SLOTQ`, and
  is the trunk encoder's half-bit clock on a fused MF8 link. **So 4.096 MHz
  never has to leave the clock card**, and the fastest edge on the
  mainboard is 1.024 MHz rather than four. Worth having: it is the one
  signal that would have wanted care.
- **`RX_OCT`.** `fabric.md` §3.4 says a card's `RX_OCT` is `CLK_8K` one
  frame late, from one flip-flop on the clock card — so it is a signal, and §2's
  table did not list it.
- **`/CON`.** §3.1 calls the control bus multi-master and leaves how the console
  takes it unsaid. One line, one '244 enable at the marker.

## 9. Not measured

- **The serial control bus on a bench.** §2.3 is a paper design: the two '164,
  the '138, the guard gate and the `WR`-to-`SCLK` separation have been counted and
  reasoned about, not scoped. **The `SCLK` glitch of §7 is the one to go looking
  for**, and the question it settles is whether bit 14 becomes parity. Scope `WR`
  against `SCLK` at the far end of the longest run, cold and warm.
- **What re-drives the scanned port's `M`.** `marker.md` §4 and
  `network.md` §3.2 both have the marker putting the probe's `M` and word out
  on the control bus so a card may watch the scan. The word is `PB1`; `M` has never
  been listed as a conductor, here or in `fabric.md` §2, and §2.3 does not add
  one. It belongs to the marker's own tap (§2.4) and wants settling in whichever of
  the three documents owns it.
- **Crosstalk.** With a ground between every pair the nearest aggressor is two
  conductors away, and published figures for interleaved ribbon put near-end
  coupling at one or two per cent; sixteen highways switching together at the
  cell boundary, dominated by the two nearest, is of the order of 5 % — a
  quarter of a volt against 74HC's volt of noise margin. **This is an estimate
  and not a measurement**, and it is the one number here that would change the
  design if it were wrong: the cure would be 1-in-1 grounding on a wider cable,
  or the economy form of `fabric.md` §6, which halves the highways to eight
  wires and is worth re-reading now that a cable, and not only packages, is what
  they cost.
- **The stub from each tap to the block's receivers** — assumed under 100 mm,
  0.6 ns, and ignored.
- **The block shunt as a stub** in the highway driver's path: 60 mm of board and
  a jumper in series with a 512 kHz signal. Nothing at this rate, unscoped.
- **Ground bounce** at the clock card, where one '244 drives fourteen lines into
  193 pF each (`RBT` is the fifteenth conductor and the clock card only
  terminates it, §2.1). The per-line current is half a milliamp; the simultaneous switch
  of eight `SLOT`/`SLOTQ` lines is not analysed.
- **Radiation.** Two metres of unshielded ribbon carrying 1.024 MHz with 59 ns
  edges, in a cabinet, next to a −48 V line feed and a 90 V ringing bus.
- **Hot-plugging a block.** The pulls make a removed block harmless (§6); a block
  inserted live, with its shunts already fitted, drives a highway other ports are
  listening to at an arbitrary instant. Not considered beyond that.
- **Everything mechanical.** No cable drawing, no board, no connector part
  numbers, nothing on a bench.

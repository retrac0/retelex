  From a 4.096 MHz crystal, counter bit C[6] is 32 kHz and C[12:7] is a 6-bit

  Two consequences worth calling out now:

  - Supervision margins at 500 Hz. The register's windows are HIT ≥ 8 ms, ID ≥
    128 ms, CLR ≥ 256 ms (register.md §2). Measured traffic: shortest break
    counted 18 ms at 20 pps, 40 ms at 7–12 pps; longest in-digit make 67 ms;
    shortest pause 255 ms. At 2 ms sampling the tightest of those is HIT — 18 ms
    is 9 samples against a 4-sample window. Comfortable everywhere else.
  - The release detectors get much smaller. They currently count 64 kHz frames,
    so 256 ms is a 15-bit counter, and at 64 ports that is 1 920 flip-flops —
    two thirds of the whole synthesised design per verilog/README.md. Counting 2
    ms passes instead makes 256 ms a count of 128: 8 bits.

  The headline: SCAN[6:0] is seven stages of the divider with no enable input,
  and tb_clock asserts on every master tick that it steps on every frame edge.
  There is no pv, no probe_dirty, no repeated visit — the stall is gone
  structurally, not by care.

  Three things worth your attention

  The polarity inverts. /HA idles high — an open-collector wire can't be pulled
  to its active state, so mainboard.md §2.2's "HA is pulled to ground: on-hook"
  no longer holds. It's commented at every tap because one inverted receiver
  leaves the exchange permanently off-hook. What it buys is that an unstuffed
  slot, an unstuffed block, a pulled card and a cut cable all read idle from one
  mechanism.

  The two-simulator rule earned its keep immediately. The mainboard's shunt
  resolution was a function called from a continuous assign; Icarus doesn't
  re-evaluate that when a signal read inside the function changes, so every
  C-path check passed under Verilator and failed under Icarus. Replaced with
  plain assigns.

  I had the release detectors wrong and you were right to stop me — they're RC
  networks, the fold doesn't touch them, and the RTL counts TICK only because
  the model has no analogue. That's said explicitly in port.v.
